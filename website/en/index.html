Why Lucet exists

Easy on a phone. Missing on the Mac.

I shoot with DJI gear. When I wanted to adjust the color and tone, I found that DJI Mimo only exists for phones (iOS and Android, as of October 2026). To edit on a big screen, I had to go back to my phone.

Lightroom and Photoshop can do anything, of course. But the first thing you see is dozens of sliders you don't understand.

Lucet aims for the middle: as simple as a phone app, on your Mac, with someone beside you to explain when you want to learn.

Features

Start simple. Go deeper when you want.

You get the handful of controls you'll use most. Everything else is still there, just tucked away.

One-click Auto

Click Auto and start from a photo that already works

Lucet looks at this photo's own brightness and color, then sets exposure, tone, white balance and color for you. It holds back:

  • Naturally bright photos, like white backdrops and snow, stay bright instead of turning gray
  • Night stays night and sunsets stay warm, so the mood survives
  • Not happy? Press ⌘Z to undo, or \ to see the original

Six sliders · Preset thumbnails

Pick a look by seeing it, not by trial and error

Every preset previews on your own photo. Click to apply; undo if you don't like it. Simple mode has just six sliders: Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Warmth and Vividness, each explained in one plain sentence.

  • AI Look: a small neural network looks at the whole photo and makes a grade just for it, computed on your Mac, nothing uploaded
  • Save your own look as a preset and apply it to other photos in one click
  • Copy one photo's adjustments to a whole batch; exposure isn't copied by default

Local adjustments

Change the sky, leave the ground alone

Often only part of a photo needs fixing: a sky that's too bright, a face that's too dark. Local adjustments limit the sliders to the area you mark:

  • Gradient: fades from one line to another, most often to darken a sky
  • Ellipse: brighten a subject, or invert it to darken the surroundings
  • Brush: adjust wherever you paint; hold ⌥ to erase
  • Subject: finds people, animals and objects in front automatically; invert it for the background
  • Each layer has its own exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, temperature, saturation, clarity, dehaze and more; “Show mask” paints its area red
  • Masks stick to the picture: crop or rotate later and they stay in place
OriginalSky gradient

One gradient: sky exposure −0.7, highlights −45, saturation +18; the ground is untouched. Real output from Lucet.

Remove

Something in the way? Paint it out

Brush over a wire, a passer-by, litter or a dust spot with Remove. When you let go, Lucet finds similar texture nearby and fills the spot in naturally.

  • Classic PatchMatch completion, computed on your Mac: no model download, nothing uploaded
  • The filled area follows your color adjustments like the rest of the photo
  • Works best on smaller things; remove large areas in a few goes
OriginalSign removed

Three brush strokes over the sign. Real output from Lucet.

Drag to crop

Drag right on the photo, just like in the Photos app

Open Crop and the whole photo appears with the crop frame on top: drag the frame to move it and a corner to resize it, and the aspect ratio holds. Straighten, rotate and flip live here too, and Lucet tells you when the horizon is tilted.

Match colors · Export a LUT

Like another photo's colors? Bring this one closer

Pick a photo in your library whose colors you like, and Lucet moves this photo's overall brightness and color toward it. The result is a LUT, so the Strength slider decides how far to go, and you can undo it.

  • It aligns the two photos' averages and spread in Lab color space (Reinhard color transfer); nothing is invented
  • It also works the other way: export your adjustments as a .cube LUT for video in DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or Premiere

Projects

One job, one project, one set of colors

Put a trip, a post or a client shoot in one project. Change the colors on any photo and the whole project follows; exposure, crop and local adjustments stay with each photo.

  • Even Out moves every photo toward the group's middle brightness and white balance, so the set looks shot together
  • “This photo” changes one picture on its own when it needs to be different
  • Export Project saves the picked photos (or all of them) into a folder named after the project
  • A photo can be in several projects, with its own version in each

Duplicates & similar shots · Compare and cull

Took several shots of the same scene? See them side by side and keep the best

Turn on “Duplicates & Similar” and Lucet groups shots with nearly the same framing and colors, plus photos the same camera took less than 2 seconds apart. Click Compare to see them side by side, press P to pick and X to reject, or choose “Keep Only This One”.

  • Files that are exactly the same (one photo imported twice, a Finder copy; videos too) come first, ready to reject or remove from the library in one click; files on disk are never deleted
  • “Zoom to 100%” zooms every photo to the same spot and pans them together, so you can tell which is sharpest
  • The comparison runs on your Mac and takes under a second for dozens of photos

Learn as you edit

Not a tutorial. A lesson about the photo in front of you.

Lucet reads the photo and points out what's really going on: where it's overexposed, where there's a color cast, how many degrees the horizon tilts, with an adjustment you can try in one click. Hover over a slider's name and the picture highlights only what it affects.

  • Interactive lessons: a few minutes each, using animation and your own photo to explain the histogram, exposure, curves, white balance, aperture, focal length, video frame rates and Log, while you try it
  • Drag any light or dark slider and the histogram marks which part it changes, from darkest to brightest
  • Choose Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced and the explanations change depth
  • Ask anything, like “Why does the sky look gray?” (optional, uses your own AI key, off by default)
  • Your learning history stays on your Mac

Save to Photos · Live Photos

Done editing? One step back to your iPhone.

Exports go straight into Photos on your Mac by default, and with iCloud Photos on they sync to your iPhone. Trim a video to 3 seconds or less and it becomes a real Live Photo. Files work too, or share directly with the system share sheet.

  • JPEG, HEIC or PNG, optionally resized for social media
  • HDR photos export with a gain map and stay HDR, edits included
  • Export presets, file-name patterns (date, number, size) and an optional text watermark
  • Capture time and camera info are kept; location is removed by default
  • Exports run in the background while you move on to the next photo
  • Originals are never changed; every export is a new copy

And also

A film look, a history you can go back through, and search by what's in the picture

  • Dehaze, Fade, Grain, Black & White in Full mode's effects; Dehaze estimates how hazy each area is with the dark-channel method
  • History: every change has a name; click one to go back to it, even after quitting
  • Search by content: type “sky”, “dog” or “sunset” in the library and photos showing it appear, recognized on your Mac

Ready for DJI footage

Edit right after shooting, and see what you shot

Lucet opens the MP4 and MOV files DJI drones and cameras produce. The Info tab lists the codec (such as HEVC Main 10), bit depth, bitrate, frame rate, color space and recording device, so you don't need another app to check.

  • Import DJI's official .cube creative LUTs; tested with the Pocket 3 Spring and Winter Pro LUTs
  • Export the colors you set in Lucet as a .cube and keep using them in DaVinci Resolve and other editors
  • Trim videos, change speed (0.5–4×), or cut 3 seconds into a Live Photo
  • 10-bit HEVC opens normally; HDR video previews, and highlight expansion is experimental

Still planned: applying the conversion LUT to D-Log footage automatically (for now you import the matching LUT yourself).

How it's made

Built with AI, one plain sentence at a time

Lucet's first version was a prototype written by ChatGPT. I handed it to Claude Code and asked it to find the problems first, then fix them step by step: large folders stopped freezing the app, the interface was rebuilt into what you see now, and Auto, preset thumbnails and learn-as-you-edit were added. This web page and the promo video were made the same way: written with web technology, then rendered.

The most interesting part: almost every change started with an ordinary sentence, like “this blue is too bright”, “not enough information” or “too many boxes”. You don't have to learn to code first. Describe the feeling clearly and let the AI try, change and test.

AI makes mistakes too, and they can look convincing. A photo of a latte on a white table was once judged “too bright overall”, with a suggestion to lower exposure by 1.5 stops, even though a white table is supposed to be bright. Problems like that showed up the moment the app was really used, and each was fixed and covered by a test.

There's a partner, too: the partner's web edition of Lucet (open source, Apache-2.0) has algorithms for grouping similar shots, matching colors and local masks, and they've been ported into this Mac app.

So Lucet would like to be built with you as well. Share ideas in GitHub Issues, and if you build things with Claude Code or other AI tools, tell us how you work.

Why learn while editing · The name

Once it looks good, you'll want to know why.

A one-click filter can make a photo look good, but next time you still won't know why.

The Douyin photography creator Zhang Xiaoyang explains histograms, curves and aperture with animation more clearly than any manual, but there are only a few videos and they can't cover all the basics. So we built that way of teaching into the app: watch your own photo's pixels pile up into a histogram, drag the aperture and see the background blur. We write and draw every lesson ourselves, and new ones keep coming.

Three steps

Import, click Auto, save

Import

Drag a folder of photos or videos into the window. Photos and videos with the same name become one Live Photo. Rate, pick and reject to find the good ones first.

Adjust

Press A for Auto, choose a preset and fine-tune with six sliders. Press \ to see the original.

Save

Save to Photos or share with friends. Come back later and every adjustment is still there, ready to undo.

Browse and organize

Thumbnails show each photo as edited. Ratings 0–5, pick and reject, filters by type and rating, and file-name search.

Full mode

Scopes (histogram, waveform, parade, vectorscope), color wheels, curves, the HSL color mixer and creative LUTs, in a fixed order with every step switchable for comparison.

Collage Live

Combine several 3-second clips into one Live Photo, with each panel graded on its own.

Undo and versions

Undo and redo any time, and keep several named versions of a photo to return to.

Is it for you?

Straight answers before you download

A good fit if you

  • Shoot with DJI or iPhone and want to make it look good on your Mac before sharing
  • Find Lightroom and Photoshop too complex and just want brightness, color and a crop
  • Want to finally understand what “highlights” and “shadows” really do
  • Like tools that never touch the original and can always undo

Not a fit yet if you

  • Need multi-track video editing, transitions or titles
  • Do in-depth RAW development or commercial-scale batch retouching
  • Use an Intel Mac or a system older than macOS 15

Privacy

Your photos stay on your Mac

Lucet has no account and no analytics, and uploads no photos. The only thing that goes online is the optional “Ask About This Photo”: it's off by default, and after you add your own key it sends a small preview (which you can turn off) and the adjustment settings only when you click Ask. Location is never included.

FAQ

You might be wondering

Will it change my original photos?

No. Every adjustment is saved as a small recipe file, and a new copy is made only when you export. Your originals, their names and locations stay as they are. If you move or rename an original in Finder, Lucet shows “Original file not found”; point it to the new location and all adjustments are kept.

What do I need to run it?

A Mac with Apple silicon (M-series) running macOS 15 or later. On macOS 26 it uses the Liquid Glass look.

Is the app in English?

Yes. Lucet follows your Mac's language and shows English or Simplified Chinese. You can also switch it under Lucet 光集 › Language · 语言 in the menu bar.

Which files does it open?

Photos: JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF, WebP, DNG and common camera RAW files (experimental). Videos: MP4, MOV and others macOS can decode. A HEIC or JPG with a same-name MOV is recognized as a Live Photo.

Can it save straight to my iPhone?

Choose “Save to Photos” when exporting; with iCloud Photos on, it syncs to your iPhone. Live Photos are saved as real Live Photos.

It says the developer can't be verified. What now?

The current version isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. That's expected; here's how to allow it:

  1. Drag Lucet 光集 into Applications, double-click it, and click Done when the warning appears
  2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll to the bottom
  3. Next to Lucet 光集, click Open Anyway and confirm

You only need to do this once. You can also build it from source; see Download below.

What is “Ask About This Photo”?

An optional feature: ask in plain language, like “How do I get a film look?”, and Claude answers based on your photo with adjustments you can try in one click. It uses your own Anthropic API key (stored in your Mac's Keychain), billed to your account, and it's off by default. Without it, everything else in Lucet works the same.
